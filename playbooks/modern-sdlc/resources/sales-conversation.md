## Connect Technical Friction to an Owned Outcome

**Example-authored sales guidance.** These prompts are not approved commercial claims or quantified return-on-investment estimates.

### Listen for the Work Being Blocked

An architecture discussion often starts with technical language: coupling, state, unit tests, or build automation. Ask what that issue prevents the customer from doing. A delay, recovery risk, or knowledge bottleneck becomes meaningful only when its context and owner are clear.

### A Conversation Structure

1. Confirm the customer's desired change and the people accountable for it.
2. Ask for a recent example of engineering friction, rather than accepting a generalized complaint.
3. Identify the impact the customer can actually measure.
4. Invite a technical review of the assumptions and constraints.
5. Agree a limited next step and an explicit decision point.

### Avoid the Shortcut

Do not claim that adopting Actor Framework, LabVIEW, containers, or CI automatically lowers development cost or improves quality. Architecture choices have learning, migration, maintenance, licensing, and operational costs. The customer must decide whether the evidence supports the proposed investment.

### Handoff to Technical Enablement

Share the agreed problem statement, constraints, acceptance evidence, and unresolved questions. Keep customer names, commercial details, and confidential project material in an approved private boundary, not this demo.
